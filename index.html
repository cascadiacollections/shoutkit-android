Privacy

No Google services. No analytics. No account.

This is the FOSS build of Shoutkit. It contains no Google Play Services, Firebase, analytics or crash reporting. Preferences and saved stations are stored on your device.

The app connects only to what it needs: the community Radio Browser directory, the stations you play, and, if you leave it on, Apple’s iTunes Search to find album art for the current song. Both the artwork lookup and Radio Browser play counts can be turned off in Settings.

Questions

Before you tune in.

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no subscription and no in-app purchases. The app is open source under the GPL-3.0, and the build on this page is free and will stay free.

Why would I pay for a store version?

You don’t have to. Store pricing hasn’t been decided. If a store build ever costs something, you’d be paying for the convenience of installing and updating through that store, and helping fund development, not unlocking features. The only differences between builds come from Google’s libraries: Chromecast and the Wear OS app need them, so they ship only in the Google Play build.

Does it track me?

No. There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting and no account. The app does talk to the services it needs: the Radio Browser directory (which by default is told when you start one of its stations, so popular stations rank higher), the stations you play, and Apple’s iTunes Search for album art. You can turn off the play counts and the artwork lookup in Settings. Details are on the privacy page.

Which stations can I listen to?

Thousands. Station search uses Radio Browser, a free, community-run directory that anyone can add to or correct. If a station isn’t listed, you can add it in Shoutkit by its stream URL.

Does it work on Android TV, Android Auto and Wear OS?

Android TV and Android Auto work in this build. The Wear OS companion and Chromecast need Google libraries, so they come with the Google Play build.

Does radio use mobile data?

Yes. Live streams need a connection and can use mobile data. Use Wi-Fi when you want to save your data allowance.
